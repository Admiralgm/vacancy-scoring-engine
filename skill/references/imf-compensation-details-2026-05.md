# IMF 2026 IT Strategist A11/A12 — Compensation & Grade Deep-Dive

## Context
User asked "what is the compensation?" after scoring the IMF IT Strategist role at 77/100. This reference captures what was learned during that research — useful for any future IMF, World Bank, or IFI role evaluation.

The IT Strategist role was hiring at A11/A12. With 30 years experience, placement at A12 top-of-band ($160-170K) is realistic.

## Tax Exemption — Critical Detail
IMF staff receive full US federal + DC income + FICA/Medicare tax exemption under IRC §893. This applies to both foreign nationals (G-4 visa) AND US citizens/permanent residents (different legal mechanism, same benefit).

**Practical math:**
- $150K IMF salary → $150K take-home (tax-free)
- $150K private sector salary → ~$93K take-home (after ~38% combined taxes in DC)
- **Effective purchasing power: $150K IMF = ~$240K private sector**

**Caveat:** Consultants (STC/ETC) do NOT get tax exemption. If the role is consultancy-based, Parameter 6 must account for this.

## Key Differences
1. **IMF pension** (30-35% DC) is significantly higher than UN agencies (15-20%). Trade-off: no defined benefit.
2. **IMF education allowance** ($30-40K/child) is among the most generous for DC-based orgs.
3. **IMF vs WB**: WB has defined benefit pension (increasingly rare), while IMF has higher DC contributions. WB base salaries slightly lower at equivalent grade.
4. **DC vs Geneva**: DC cost of living is higher than Geneva. Geneva UN staff have equivalent tax exemption but lower housing costs and better school access.
